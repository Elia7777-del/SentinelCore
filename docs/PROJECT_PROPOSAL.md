## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SentinelCore is an intelligent, centralized cybersecurity platform designed to provide continuous 24/7 monitoring,
detection, prevention, investigation, containment, response, recovery support, and security reporting for
organizational networks, endpoints, applications, identities, cloud environments, and data.

The central workflow is:

**MONITOR → COLLECT → NORMALIZE → DETECT → CORRELATE → ANALYZE → SCORE → DECIDE → RESPOND → VERIFY → EXPLAIN → RECORD → REPORT → IMPROVE**

The platform is sector-neutral and adaptable to government institutions, national agencies, banks, financial
institutions, insurance companies, healthcare organizations, universities, telecommunications companies,
manufacturers, retailers, technology companies, NGOs, SMEs, and other authorized organizations.

SentinelCore may combine capabilities commonly associated with SIEM, IDS/IPS, endpoint monitoring, threat
intelligence, UEBA, AI/ML analytics, SOAR, incident management, threat hunting, digital forensics, asset
management, IAM, Zero Trust integration, vulnerability management, and security reporting.

SentinelCore does not claim automatic certification, universal detection, or guaranteed acceptance by every
organization or government institution. Production use requires appropriate engineering, security testing,
legal/regulatory review, authorization, procurement, independent assessment, and certification/accreditation
where applicable.

## 2. PROJECT BACKGROUND

Digital transformation has increased dependence on networks, cloud infrastructure, databases, applications,
mobile devices, online services, payment systems, remote access, IoT and digital communications. This
increases the attack surface and creates a need for continuous security visibility.

## 3. PROJECT JUSTIFICATION

The project is justified by the need to improve continuous monitoring, threat visibility, detection speed,
incident response, automation, asset visibility, vulnerability management, reporting, investigation,
compliance evidence, and cyber resilience.

## 4. PROBLEM STATEMENT

Organizations face continuous threats, delayed detection, fragmented security infrastructure, alert overload,
slow response, unknown assets, vulnerable systems, limited cybersecurity expertise, and complex multi-stage attacks.

## 5. PROPOSED SOLUTION

SentinelCore provides centralized security monitoring and analysis across prevention, detection, response,
investigation, and governance.

## 6. PROJECT AIM

To develop an intelligent, reliable, secure, scalable and explainable cybersecurity platform capable of
continuously monitoring organizational environments, detecting and prioritizing threats, supporting approved
containment, assisting investigation, preserving evidence, and providing actionable security intelligence 24/7.

## 8. PROJECT SCOPE

Network security, endpoint security, identity security, application/cloud monitoring, vulnerability management,
threat intelligence, analytics, incident management, automated response, threat hunting, digital forensics,
reporting, compliance support, asset management, and security awareness.

The system is for authorized defensive cybersecurity use and is not designed for unauthorized attacks,
credential theft, or compromise of third-party systems.

## 9. TARGET ORGANIZATIONS AND USERS

Government, national agencies, financial institutions, banks, insurance, healthcare, education, telecommunications,
manufacturing, retail, technology organizations, NGOs, SMEs, SOC teams, security analysts, administrators,
responders, auditors and executives.

## 10. SECURITY PRINCIPLES

Confidentiality, integrity, availability, least privilege, defense in depth, Zero Trust, secure by design,
privacy by design, continuous monitoring, risk-based decisions, human oversight, accountability and resilience.

## 11. CORE SECURITY CAPABILITIES

SIEM-style event management, IDS/IPS integration, endpoint monitoring, threat intelligence, vulnerability
management, UEBA, AI/ML analytics, SOAR-style orchestration, incident management, threat hunting, digital
forensics, asset management, IAM, Zero Trust integration and security reporting.

## 12. THREAT LANDSCAPE AND THREAT COVERAGE

Coverage targets malware, ransomware, phishing, credential theft, brute force, unauthorized access, privilege
escalation, lateral movement, data exfiltration, suspicious network activity, DoS indicators, insider threats,
vulnerability exploitation, malicious scripts, suspicious processes, abnormal authentication and C2 indicators.

Coverage will be measured against explicitly implemented and validated detections rather than claiming universal detection.

## 13. 24/7 MONITORING

SentinelCore continuously evaluates supported networks, endpoints, servers, applications, users, authentication
systems, cloud services, databases and security devices. True 24/7 human monitoring can be provided through an
internal SOC, external SOC or managed security service.

## 14. ASSET DISCOVERY AND ASSET INVENTORY

Maintain hostname, IP, device type, operating system, applications, owner, location where appropriate, security
status, vulnerabilities, criticality, risk and activity information. Assets may be classified Critical, High,
Medium or Low.

## 15. DATA COLLECTION AND NORMALIZATION

Collect authorized events from firewalls, routers, switches, servers, endpoints, applications, databases,
identity systems, cloud platforms and security tools, then normalize them into a common event structure.

## 16. THREAT DETECTION ENGINE

Detection layers include signature-based, rule-based, anomaly, behavioral, intelligence-based and correlation detection.

## 17. EVENT CORRELATION AND ANALYSIS

Correlate time, user, device, IP, domain, process, hash, application, authentication, threat intelligence and
asset criticality to identify multi-stage patterns.

## 18. RISK SCORING AND PRIORITIZATION

Risk may consider threat severity, asset criticality, detection confidence, exposure, vulnerability and user risk.
A conceptual model is:

**Risk = Threat Severity × Asset Criticality × Exposure × Detection Confidence**

Scores may be Critical, High, Medium, Low or Informational. Final algorithms require validation.

## 19. THREAT INTELLIGENCE

Support authorized intelligence feeds containing IPs, domains, URLs, hashes, malware indicators, techniques and
other validated indicators.

## 20. USER AND ENTITY BEHAVIOR ANALYTICS

Establish behavioral baselines for users, servers, endpoints, applications and network devices to identify
unusual logins, data transfers, administration, process execution and communication.

## 21. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND MACHINE LEARNING

AI/ML may assist anomaly detection, behavioral analytics, correlation, prioritization, classification and
false-positive reduction. AI is an analytical component, not an unquestionable authority. High-impact actions
remain subject to policy and human oversight where required.

## 22. THREAT HUNTING

Authorized analysts can search historical data for suspicious destinations, authentication patterns,
administrative activity, processes and data transfers that may not have generated traditional alerts.

## 23. INCIDENT MANAGEMENT

Lifecycle:

**Detected → Triaged → Investigating → Contained → Remediating → Resolved → Closed**

Incidents may include severity, risk, assets, evidence, timeline, actions, notes, resolution and lessons learned.

## 24. AUTOMATED RESPONSE AND CONTAINMENT

Support policy-controlled blocking, quarantine, endpoint isolation, account restriction, incident creation,
notifications and playbooks. High-impact actions require explicit policy and may require human approval.

## 25. HUMAN OVERSIGHT AND RESPONSE GOVERNANCE

Four response levels are supported conceptually:

1. Alert Only
2. Recommend Action
3. Automatic Containment
4. Human Authorization

Critical production and government systems may use stricter approval requirements.

## 26. DIGITAL FORENSICS AND EVIDENCE MANAGEMENT

Support authorized collection and organization of logs, authentication records, processes, hashes, network
connections, endpoint events and timelines. Evidence protection may use hashes, timestamps, collector identity,
access history and chain-of-custody records.

## 27. EXPLAINABLE SECURITY ACTIONS

Important decisions should explain what was detected, when, affected asset/user, reason, rule/model contribution,
risk, recommended action, action taken and authorization.

## 28. IDENTITY AND ACCESS MANAGEMENT

Support RBAC, MFA, least privilege, provisioning/deprovisioning, session management, password policies,
privileged access controls and access reviews.

## 29. ZERO-TRUST SECURITY ARCHITECTURE

Support continuous verification using identity, device posture, authentication strength, resource sensitivity,
access context, risk and behavior. SentinelCore complements rather than replaces Zero Trust technologies.

## 30. VULNERABILITY AND CONFIGURATION MANAGEMENT

Support missing updates, vulnerable software, unsupported systems, weak configurations, exposed services and
excessive privileges. Dedicated vulnerability scanners may be integrated for deeper assessment.

## 31. DATA PROTECTION AND PRIVACY

Protect security information with encryption, access control, classification, retention, secure deletion,
audit logging and backups. Processing of personal/sensitive information must follow applicable laws and
organizational requirements.

## 32. SECURITY OF SENTINELCORE ITSELF

Use secure development, strong authentication, MFA, RBAC, secure APIs, secrets management, vulnerability
scanning, code review, security testing, monitoring and protected backups.

## 33. LOGGING, AUDITING AND EVIDENCE INTEGRITY

Maintain audit records for authentication, administration, configuration changes, detections, response actions,
evidence access, incidents and policy changes. Protect records using hashing, append-only storage and restricted access.

## 34. SECURITY OPERATIONS CENTER INTEGRATION

Support internal SOCs, managed security services, sector SOCs and national/organizational SOC environments through
monitoring, triage, investigation, hunting, response and reporting.

## 35. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Logical layers:

**Data Sources → Collectors → Ingestion/Normalization → Detection → Correlation/Risk → Incident Management →
Response Orchestration → Verification/Evidence/Audit → Dashboards/Reports/SOC Integration**

## 36. INTEGRATION AND INTEROPERABILITY

Potential integrations include firewalls, IDS/IPS, endpoint security, Active Directory, identity providers,
cloud platforms, email security, network devices, databases, applications, vulnerability scanners, ticketing
systems and threat-intelligence feeds through APIs, syslog, webhooks, agents and message queues.

## 37. SCALABILITY AND HIGH AVAILABILITY

Use distributed collectors, horizontal scaling, load balancing, distributed processing, storage expansion,
modular services, cloud/hybrid deployment, redundancy, failover, replication and health monitoring.

## 38. DISASTER RECOVERY AND BUSINESS CONTINUITY

Support backups, off-site storage, configuration backups, failover and recovery testing. Organizations define
RTO and RPO according to business requirements.

## 39. COMPLIANCE AND STANDARDS ALIGNMENT

The platform may be mapped to NIST Cybersecurity Framework, NIST security controls, ISO/IEC 27001, CIS Controls,
OWASP practices, MITRE ATT&CK and applicable national/sector requirements. Alignment does not automatically mean certification.

## 40. GOVERNMENT AND NATIONAL-LEVEL READINESS

Government deployment requires applicable laws, policies, procurement, data protection, classification,

## 41. ENTERPRISE DEPLOYMENT MODELS

On-premises, private cloud, public cloud, hybrid and managed-security deployments may be supported.

## 42. USER TRAINING AND SECURITY AWARENESS

Training covers platform administration, alert investigation, incident response, threat hunting, dashboards,
evidence management, reporting, playbooks and general cybersecurity awareness.

## 47. SECURITY ASSURANCE AND INDEPENDENT ASSESSMENT

## 52. PROJECT RISK MANAGEMENT

Major risks include funding, technical complexity, false positives/negatives, data quality, integration,

## 53. MINIMUM HARDWARE AND INFRASTRUCTURE REQUIREMENTS

high-performance storage, redundancy and disaster recovery according to event volume.

## 54. MAINTENANCE AND LIFECYCLE MANAGEMENT

## 56. PROJECT DELIVERABLES

Software platform, architecture documentation, database design, security architecture, deployment documentation,

## 57. CONCLUSION

SentinelCore is proposed as a comprehensive cybersecurity platform that coordinates monitoring, detection,
analysis, prioritization, investigation, containment, response, evidence management and reporting.

Its objective is to transform cybersecurity operations from fragmented and reactive processes into a more
centralized, intelligence-driven, measurable and continuously improving capability.

Successful deployment will depend on engineering quality, cybersecurity testing, infrastructure, governance,
authorization, skilled personnel, regulatory compliance, independent assessment and continuous improvement.
